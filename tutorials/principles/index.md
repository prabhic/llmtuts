# Article Writing Principles (Reference)

A concise, reusable checklist distilled from Meta Principles for Writing About Emerging Technologies. Use this as a quick reference when drafting any new article.

## Voice & Stance
- Honest explorer: share surprises, uncertainties, and active learning.
- Hype inoculation: separate capability from maturity; call out marketing vs. reality.
- Peer-to-peer tone: write like to a colleague; keep paragraphs tight.

## Structure
- 30-second hook: lead with a unique insight and why it matters now.
- Progressive value: each section stands alone and ends with a takeaway.
- Conceptual before implementation: explain the mental model shift first.
- Honest uncertainty: include open questions and what you’re watching.

## Reader-Centric
- Multi-altitude: product, architecture, and developer implications per insight.
- Decision framework: when to explore, skip, or wait and watch.
- Monday-morning actions: concrete, low-risk next steps and prompts.

## Visuals & Format
- Scannable depth: headings → callouts → brief paragraphs → bold takeaway.
- Conceptual visuals: show trade-offs and mental models, not step-by-steps.

## Credibility & Trust
- Embedded evidence: provenance, scope, assumptions, and caveats.
- No-BS honesty: call constraints and trade-offs explicitly.
- Founder’s lens: balance technical elegance with business pragmatism.

## Emerging Tech Signals
- State maturity: early exploration vs. concepts solidifying.
- Evolution context: where it comes from and what it enables next.
- Trade-off transparency: when it’s worth it, when it isn’t.

## Quick Templates
### Opening
> [One-sentence unique observation]. I’ve been exploring [tech] not because it’s production‑ready, but because [architectural insight]. Here’s what I’m learning that may change how you think about [domain].

### Section Close
**Key Insight:** [One sentence]

🎯 Product: [Strategic implication]
🏗️ Architecture: [Design implication]
⚡ Dev: [Tactical implication]

**Question:** [Provocative, context‑applying prompt]

### Trade-off Block
Tension: [X vs. Y]. Worth it when [conditions]. Not worth it when [anti‑patterns]. Open question: [unknown].
